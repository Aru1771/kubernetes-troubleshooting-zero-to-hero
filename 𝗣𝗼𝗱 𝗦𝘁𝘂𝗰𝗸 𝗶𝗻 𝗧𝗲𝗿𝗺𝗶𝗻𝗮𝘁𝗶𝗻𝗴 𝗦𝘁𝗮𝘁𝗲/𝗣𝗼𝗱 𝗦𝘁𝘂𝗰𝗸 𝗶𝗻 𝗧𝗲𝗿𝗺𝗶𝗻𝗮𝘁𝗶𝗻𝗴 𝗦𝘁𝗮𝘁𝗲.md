Today I spent some time understanding why a Kubernetes Pod sometimes gets stuck in the Terminating state.
Initially, I assumed it was just a temporary issue and that Kubernetes would eventually delete the pod. But later,
I realized that a pod can remain in the Terminating state because Kubernetes is waiting for something to complete before removing it.

-----------------------------------------------
✅ The application doesn't shut down gracefully after receiving 𝗦𝗜𝗚𝗧𝗘𝗥𝗠
✅ Persistent Volumes (𝓟𝓥/𝓟𝓥𝓒) are still attached and can't be unmounted
✅ Finalizers are blocking the deletion process
✅ Kubelet or container runtime issues
✅ A long 𝙩𝙚𝙧𝙢𝙞𝙣𝙖𝙩𝙞𝙤𝙣𝙂𝙧𝙖𝙘𝙚𝙋𝙚𝙧𝙞𝙤𝙙𝙎𝙚𝙘𝙤𝙣𝙙𝙨 value

➡️ 𝗜𝘀 𝗳𝗼𝗿𝗰𝗲 𝗱𝗲𝗹𝗲𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝗻𝗲𝗰𝗲𝘀𝘀𝗮𝗿𝘆❓
One important lesson I learned is : 𝗸𝘂𝗯𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗹 𝗱𝗲𝗹𝗲𝘁𝗲 𝗽𝗼𝗱 --𝗳𝗼𝗿𝗰𝗲 should never be the first option.
Force deleting a pod may hide the real problem instead of fixing it. It's much better to understand why Kubernetes is unable to delete the pod before using force deletion as a last resort.

Every troubleshooting issue teaches me something new about how Kubernetes works internally. 
Documenting these scenarios has been a great way to strengthen my DevOps skills and prepare for real production environments.
